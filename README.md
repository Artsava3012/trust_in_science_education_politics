The thesis explores the associative patterns between trust in science and scientists and political regimes across different educational groups in pre- and intra-pandemic periods.
RQ1: What was the association between political regime and trust in science and scientists across different educational cohorts in the pre-pandemic era?
RQ2: How did the relationship between political regime and trust in science and scientists change during the Covid-19 pandemic across different educational cohorts?
Theory: Revised Modernisation Theory & Reflexive Modernisation provisions (Inglehart & Welzel, 2008; Beck, 1992; Beck et al., 1997), Merton’s theory (1973), Legitimization theory (2023)
Data: Wellcome Global Monitor (2018/2020), V-Dem Institute, Worldwide Governance Indicators, World Bank, IMF
Methods: Ordered Logistic Regression (OLR) & Generalized Additive Models (GAM)
Findings:
1. The relationship between the political regime and trust in science and scientists is nonlinear and cohort-specific
2. While partial democratization is associated with a decline in trust (primary, secondary), the transition to full-fledged liberal democracy is connected with an increase in trust (secondary)
3. In a crisis situation, education plays a more important role in a liberal democracy than in an autocracy in “predicting” trust.
4. No theory fully describes the observed patterns
